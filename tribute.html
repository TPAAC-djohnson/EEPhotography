Through Her Lens

Honoring Black Women Who Documented Truth, Culture, and Humanity

Introduction

Black women photographers have long used their cameras as tools to preserve history, culture, and lived experiences. Through documentary and narrative photography, they have captured moments that might otherwise be lost to time, giving voice to communities and stories that deserve to be seen and heard.

Featured Photographers

Carrie Mae Weems

Carrie Mae Weems is renowned for her narrative photography that explores themes of identity, family, and history. Through staged and documentary images, she interrogates the ways in which race and gender shape our understanding of the past and present.

Narrative photography, identity & history

Photographers of the Civil Rights Era

During the Civil Rights era, numerous Black women photographers collaborated with peers like Gordon Parks to document the struggle for equality. Their images of marches, protests, and everyday life played a crucial role in shaping public awareness and preserving historical memory.

Documentary photography, civil rights documentation

LaToya Ruby Frazier

LaToya Ruby Frazier uses photography and video to explore the impact of industry and economic decline on her hometown of Braddock, Pennsylvania. Her powerful images document community resilience and the ongoing struggles of working-class families.

Documentary storytelling about community & industry

Lorna Simpson

Lorna Simpson is a conceptual artist whose work combines photography, text, and installation to probe issues of race, gender, and identity. Her evocative images challenge viewers to question their own assumptions and the ways in which photographs convey meaning.

Conceptual photography about race & gender

Deborah Willis

Historian and photographer Deborah Willis has dedicated her career to documenting Black culture and everyday life. Her images and scholarship have unearthed countless stories of African American photographers and the communities they portray.

Historical documentation of Black culture

Ming Smith

As the first Black woman photographer to join the Kamoinge Workshop, Ming Smith is known for her impressionistic style and dynamic use of blur and light. Her photographs capture the spirit of everyday moments with poetic sensitivity.

First Black female photographer in the Kamoinge Workshop

Storytelling in Photography

Documentary photography does more than record events—it preserves social history and amplifies voices that might otherwise remain unheard. By capturing the nuances of daily life and moments of activism, Black women photographers provide a lens through which we can understand our collective past and envision a more equitable future.

Inspiration

“Photography is a potent medium for influencing the way we look at the world, the way we see ourselves, and the way we see others.” — Deborah Willis
“Our responsibility is to create images that speak to what we want to see in the world.” — Carrie Mae Weems

Our Connection

Eve’s Eden Photography draws inspiration from these pioneering storytellers. Our mission is to continue the tradition of using imagery to celebrate diversity, illuminate truth, and capture the essence of the human experience. By honoring those who came before us, we strive to contribute to a more inclusive visual culture.
