# ROOM SERVICE — Gallery Layout and Final Image Titles

**Artist:** Darryl Johnson  
**Series:** ROOM SERVICE  
**Subtitle:** She Is Alone, But Never Off Stage  
**Exhibition Format:** Intimate fine art photography installation  
**Recommended Works:** 10 final images

---

## Curatorial Thesis

ROOM SERVICE unfolds like a private emotional room. The viewer enters through beauty, moves through pressure, witnesses collapse, and leaves with voice returning. The exhibition should not feel like a collection of portraits. It should feel like a private interior becoming visible.

The works should be experienced in three movements:

1. **Entrance / Invitation** — the viewer is allowed in.
2. **Weight / Interior Pressure** — the private room reveals its emotional cost.
3. **Voice / Return** — the subject reclaims authorship and exits as presence, not spectacle.

# Physical Gallery Layout

## Recommended Room Shape

A rectangular or L-shaped room works best. The exhibition should feel like a private domestic interior rather than a traditional gallery corridor.

## Entry Wall

### Work 01 — Permission to Enter

Place this directly visible from the entrance. This work should function as the threshold.

**Recommended size:** 24 x 36 in.  
**Placement:** Centered at eye level, with generous negative space.

---

## Left Wall — Invitation and Awareness

### Work 02 — She Knows the Room Is Watching
### Work 03 — Internal Weight

These works should hang with enough distance for the viewer to feel watched, then pulled closer.

**Recommended size:** 18 x 24 in. or 24 x 36 in.  
**Spacing:** 24–30 inches between frames.

---

## Center Wall — Interior Pressure

### Work 04 — The Distance Between Sound and Silence
### Work 05 — After the Performance
### Work 06 — The Flame Kept Watch

This is the heaviest section. The works should be closer together than the entrance wall, creating compression and emotional pressure.

**Recommended size:**
- Work 04: 24 x 36 in.
- Work 05: 18 x 24 in.
- Work 06: 24 x 36 in.

**Layout:** Work 05 should sit between 04 and 06 as the collapse point.

---

## Right Wall — Return of Voice

### Work 07 — Voice Returning
### Work 08 — Soft Armor
### Work 09 — What She Withheld

This wall should feel like recovery, agency, and controlled distance. The subject moves from sound to self-protection.

**Recommended size:** 18 x 24 in. or 24 x 36 in.

---

## Exit Wall

### Work 10 — Not for Consumption

This image should stand alone, with no competing works nearby. It is the final refusal and final authority.

**Recommended size:** 24 x 36 in. minimum.  
**Placement:** Centered, isolated, quiet.

---

# Installation Notes

## Wall Text

Place only one main wall text near the entrance.

**Suggested wall text:**

ROOM SERVICE  
She Is Alone, But Never Off Stage

This series explores the private emotional and psychological environments of Black women within intimate, domestic spaces. Each image exists between authenticity and performance, where presence and perception quietly collide.

## Lighting

- Warm gallery lighting, approximately 3000K.
- Angle lights at roughly 30 degrees.
- Avoid harsh glare on glass.
- Keep the center wall slightly dimmer to intensify the emotional weight.

## Sound

Optional: low room tone, soft ambient hum, or distant breath-like texture. Avoid lyrical music. The room should feel alive, not decorated.

## Physical Object

One small table with a glass, folded fabric, or candle-like object may be placed near the center wall. Keep it minimal. The object should feel like residue from the images, not a prop display.

---

# Collector Wall Strategy

Place a discreet collector card near the exit, not the entrance. Let viewers experience the work before being invited to acquire it.

**Collector card text:**

Selected works from ROOM SERVICE are available as limited archival pigment prints. Collector inquiries may be directed to Cans1r@eepatl.com.

---

# Final Curatorial Note

The strongest version of ROOM SERVICE is not loud. It is controlled, intimate, and slightly uncomfortable. The viewer should feel invited, implicated, and then dismissed. The final image must make clear that the subject is not available for consumption. She allowed the viewer to witness, not possess.
